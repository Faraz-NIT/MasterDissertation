Executive summary
Retail replenishment connects sales expectations with stock, supplier conditions and spending 
decisions. This dissertation asks whether a small local large language model can help that 
process and what it adds beyond a capable numerical planner. It follows the progression of 
the supplied Gilbert thesis, while using a newly written retail narrative and the completed no-
repair Ollama study as its empirical foundation.
The  study  uses  thirty  selected  store–product  series  from  M5  and  thirty  from 
FreshRetailNet-50K, called RetailNet in the graphs. Three language-model roles read current 
supplier messages, request a planning tool and review its checks. Two reference policies 
provide the comparisons: conventional ordering and the same structured-data planner without 
language-model roles. Two historical weeks, three operating situations and thirty simulation 
seeds produce 1,080 completed runs and 7,560 fresh model calls.
Compared with conventional ordering, the complete workflow increased simulated demand 
fulfilment by 25.28 percentage points in M5 and 0.78 points in RetailNet. The paired 
inventory operating-cost reductions were 70.34% and 4.52%. These measures concern a 
disclosed simulator and synthetic cost assumptions. They are not measured retailer profits or 
production savings.
The language model read all controlled supplier messages accurately. Business-priority 
selection was less reliable, especially during RetailNet promotions. The same numerical 
planner performed almost identically in M5 and slightly better in RetailNet, so it explains 
most of the inventory-performance improvement. The useful demonstrated LLM role is 
supplier-information  interpretation  within  a  bounded  process;  an  additional  numerical 
replenishment advantage was not established.
The experiments ran on Linux with four allocated CPU cores, 32 GiB memory and no GPU. 
Qwen2.5 1.5B was pretrained and served locally through Ollama, with no retail fine-tuning. 
LightGBM was fitted to past retail histories to supply the common forecast. The managerial 
recommendation is to retain a capable planner and explicit policy rules, then test language 
assistance  where  interpreting  incoming  information  creates  a  real  workload.  Staff-time 
savings, implementation cost and commercial return remain questions for an operational 
pilot.

1. Retail replenishment and the business case for change
1.1 A daily decision with consequences beyond the shelf
Retail replenishment begins with a practical question: how much should a store order today? 
Behind that question sit several competing expectations. Customers expect the product to be 
available. The retailer wants to avoid tying up money in stock that moves slowly. Suppliers 
need orders that respect delivery arrangements and carton sizes. For fresh products, the store 
also needs to sell the goods before they expire. An order can satisfy one expectation while 
making another harder to achieve. Buying more may protect availability, for example, but it 
can increase the amount that remains unsold.
The  decision  is  repeated  across  many  products  and  locations.  A  store  manager  might 
understand why one product needs extra stock, yet struggle to give the same attention to 
hundreds of order lines. Routine work includes checking what is already on the shelf, 
identifying deliveries that are still on the way, reviewing expected sales and applying supplier 
conditions.  The  workload  grows  when  different  suppliers  express  those  conditions 
differently. The numerical  order is therefore the end of a longer process of gathering 
information, interpreting it and deciding which rules apply.
Inventory research has long treated this problem as a balance between service and cost. 
Zipkin  (2000)  explains  how  demand  uncertainty,  delivery  delays  and  the  treatment  of 
shortages shape the choice of inventory policy. These ideas remain relevant when a retailer 
introduces artificial intelligence. A language model does not remove the need to understand 
the delivery process or the cost of carrying stock. It changes one part of the information and 
decision  process,  which  must  still  operate  within  the  retailer's  economic  and  physical 
constraints.
For a business school dissertation, the useful starting point is the decision itself. The question 
is  whether  a  new  arrangement  helps  the  retailer  serve  demand  and  manage  inventory 
responsibly. A technically impressive model can still be difficult to use, slow to respond or 
poorly aligned with an operating policy. Conversely, a modest model may be useful if it 
performs a clearly defined task reliably. This dissertation examines that second possibility: a 
small  local  language  model  working  alongside  conventional  forecasting  and  numerical 
planning tools.

1.2 Why forecasting alone does not settle the order
A forecast estimates future sales. Replenishment turns that estimate into a purchase decision. 
The distinction matters because a good sales prediction can still lead to an unsuitable order. 
The store may already hold enough inventory, a delivery may be due tomorrow, or the 
proposed quantity may break a supplier's minimum-order rule. A decision that ignores these 
conditions can look sensible when considered only against the forecast. It becomes less 
sensible when the receiving team, supplier or finance department tries to execute it.
Consider an illustrative grocery store preparing for a busy weekend. Its forecast suggests that 
sales of a product will increase. The supplier sells that product in cartons of six and requires 
at least two cartons for an order. An additional delivery delay means that some of the forecast 
demand will occur before a new order can arrive. The manager needs to recognise these facts 
together. An order for seven units is unavailable under the carton rule. An order that arrives 
after the weekend also cannot satisfy the demand that occurs before its arrival. Neither 
problem is solved by improving the forecast alone.
Shared supplier capacity creates another complication. Several products can all need stock on 
the same day, while the supplier has a limit on what it can dispatch. The retailer then has to 
decide how to distribute the available capacity. Giving every product the same proportion of 
its requested quantity is understandable and easy to explain. It may, however, allocate stock 
differently from a planner that considers which shortages are most urgent. The allocation rule 
can influence customer service as much as the quality of the underlying sales estimate.
Van Donselaar et al. (2010) place automated replenishment in the context of actual ordering 
behaviour. Their work helps explain why the introduction of a system is also a change in a 
business  process.  Retailers  need  to  decide  how  the  recommendation  fits  with  local 
knowledge, working routines and managerial responsibility. The present dissertation takes 
that process perspective. It evaluates a workflow that combines a shared forecast, an ordering 
tool and language-model roles, while retaining clear checks before an order is released.

1.3 The attraction of language models in this setting
Retail information does not always arrive in the format expected by a planning system. A 
supplier may send a short email announcing a new carton size. Another may provide a table 
of delivery conditions. A third may put the dispatch limit at the beginning of a memo and the 
minimum order at the end. People can usually recognise that these documents describe 
related business rules. A conventional planning tool, however, needs those rules in consistent 
fields before it can calculate a quantity. Converting a message into usable information is a 
plausible role for a language model.
The attraction is wider than document reading. A language model can also connect a business 
instruction  to  an  approved  tool.  If  a  retailer  announces  a  promotion  and  decides  that 
availability should take priority, a coordination role can request the planning mode that 
reflects that instruction. A review role can examine the results of independent checks and 
return an approval or a hold. These are bounded tasks within a workflow. They let the model 
contribute  through  interpretation  and  coordination  while  the  numerical  tool  remains 
responsible for arithmetic and the business constraints remain explicit.
This arrangement is relevant to managers because it resembles the division of work in a retail 
operation. A person who receives supplier information does not necessarily calculate every 
order quantity. A planner uses that information with stock and demand estimates. A reviewer 
then checks  that  the  proposal  can  be  released. The  experimental  system  assigns  these 
responsibilities to separate roles. All three language-model roles use the same underlying 
pretrained model, rather than three separately trained models. The distinction between a role 
and a model is important when interpreting both capability and computing requirements.
The appeal should also be examined carefully. A language model may produce a well-formed 
answer that contains a wrong number or selects the wrong business priority. A fluent 
response can make the error easy to miss. Successful document reading therefore does not 
automatically establish successful replenishment. The experiment measures each of these 
responsibilities separately and then assesses the consequences of the complete workflow. 
This allows the dissertation to discuss where the model helped without assuming that every 
successful inventory outcome was caused by the language-model component.
1.4 The management problem behind the research
The practical management problem is how to introduce language-model assistance without 
losing the strengths of existing replenishment methods. Retailers already have approaches for 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 9 of 70
estimating demand and turning that estimate into stock targets. Replacing those approaches 
simply because a conversational model is available would require a convincing reason. A 
more measured approach is to identify a task where language understanding may be useful, 
connect that task to an established planner, and assess whether the resulting process maintains 
or improves operational performance.
This raises an attribution question. Suppose an agent-assisted system fulfils more demand 
than a simple ordering rule. The improvement could come from better allocation, more 
careful treatment of arriving stock or a different service priority. The language model may 
have contributed by reading the supplier message correctly, yet the numerical planner may 
explain most of the improvement. Managers need this distinction when considering an 
investment. Otherwise, a useful planning improvement can be presented as evidence for an 
additional model component whose value has not actually been measured.
The study therefore includes two comparisons. Conventional ordering provides an accessible 
reference for the performance of the complete package. A structured-data planner supplies 
the same numerical tool and current business rules without a language model. Its information 
is already organised in the fields that the tool needs. This second comparison asks whether 
adding the language-model roles changes the outcome once the planning capability is held 
constant. It is demanding, but it gives the research a clearer basis for explaining what the 
model adds.
The structured-data comparison also has a business limitation. In a real retailer, someone or 
some software would need to convert incoming messages into those fields. The experiment 
does not measure that effort, and it does not assume that structured information appears 
without cost in practice. The comparison isolates inventory performance, while the potential 
value of reducing manual interpretation remains a question for an operational pilot. This 
separation keeps an attractive business possibility visible without presenting unmeasured staff 
savings as an experimental result.
1.5 Research purpose and questions
The  purpose  of  this  dissertation  is  to  evaluate  whether  a  small  local  language-model 
workflow can participate reliably in retail replenishment, and to identify the contribution of 
that workflow relative to simpler and stronger references. The focus is a complete order 
process with supplier reading, planning coordination and review. The system is evaluated on 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 10 of 70
selected retail sales series from M5 and FreshRetailNet-50K. FreshRetailNet is shortened to 
RetailNet in the figures so that the dataset labels remain easy to read.
The first research question concerns the complete workflow: how does an Ollama-based 
agent system perform against conventional ordering in customer-demand fulfilment and an 
inventory operating-cost measure? The second asks about the language-model component: 
what changes when the same planning tool operates with language-model roles instead of 
already structured inputs and a fixed priority rule? The third concerns reliability and context: 
how accurately do the roles interpret supplier rules and select business priorities, and how do 
the findings differ between the two retail settings?
These questions connect business performance to the mechanism that produces it. The first 
can identify whether the overall design is worth considering. The second can prevent an 
incorrect attribution of its benefits. The third helps identify which responsibilities need 
further attention. Together they are more informative than a single comparison that reports 
the total cost of an agent system and treats every reduction as proof of superior artificial 
intelligence. They also make it possible to report a useful outcome when the strongest 
evidence concerns interpretation rather than better numerical ordering.
The research is evaluative rather than a claim of commercial deployment. It develops a 
defined system, applies it to historical data in a disclosed simulator and assesses the resulting 
evidence. There are no retailer interviews, staff observations or live purchase orders in the 
study. The findings concern the selected panels and operating assumptions. This boundary 
does not remove their relevance. It establishes what a manager can reasonably learn from the 
experiment and what would still need to be measured before making a production investment.
1.6 Two datasets, one common decision framework
M5 and RetailNet offer different settings in which to examine the same decision process. M5 
provides a long history of daily retail sales and an established forecasting research context. 
RetailNet provides a fresh-retail setting with normalized sales and published information 
about stockouts. Both allow the construction of store–product sales series, yet they differ in 
their history, quantity conventions and the practical issues they highlight. Using both gives 
the dissertation a broader view than a single demonstration, while keeping the experiment 
small enough for local CPU inference.
The  performance  panel  contains  thirty  selected  series  from  each  dataset.  This  is  a 
computational study of sixty series in total, rather than an evaluation of every series in either

source. The selection uses information available before the evaluation periods. Each dataset is 
assessed over two separate historical weeks, three operating situations and thirty simulation 
seeds. The same forecasting family and the same matched operating conditions are used 
across the three policies within a case. The design is intended to make policy differences 
interpretable rather than to compare unrelated settings.
In the simulator, M5 stock does not expire within the weekly horizon. RetailNet stock is 
assigned a hypothetical three-day usable life and consumed in first-in, first-out order. The 
shelf-life assumption adds an explicit availability–waste trade-off; it is not an observed 
product-level attribute supplied by the dataset. The raw quantity conventions also differ. M5 
uses published unit sales, while RetailNet quantities are represented as published normalized 
sales multiplied by one hundred. Relative effects within a dataset are consequently more 
meaningful than a comparison of their absolute purchasing volumes.
The common framework helps identify both shared and context-specific findings. Supplier 
reading  can  be  evaluated  in  the  same  controlled  formats  in  both  datasets.  Inventory 
performance can then be interpreted in relation to the selected sales profiles and the declared 
physical assumptions. A stronger result in M5 does not mean that M5 is a better dataset, or 
that the language model is generally more intelligent in that setting. It means that the 
measured workflow produced a different effect under those particular demand patterns, 
reference policies and operating conditions.
1.7 Contribution and organisation of the dissertation
The intended contribution is a clear account of what a local language-model replenishment 
workflow can do, how that performance was measured and how its benefits should be 
attributed. The study connects operational outcomes with role-level behaviour rather than 
treating the agent system as an unexplained package. It also retains a strong reference that 
shares  the  numerical  planning  tool.  This  allows  the  positive  evidence  for  supplier 
interpretation to be discussed alongside the limits of the measured inventory advantage from 
adding the language-model roles.
The dissertation follows the broad progression of the user-supplied master's thesis by Gilbert 
(2025):  problem  context,  the  role  of  data,  the  relevance  of  a  model  family,  research 
development,  findings,  implications  and  future  research.  The  reference  is  used  for 
organisation  and  presentation.  Its  climate  topic,  empirical  claims,  figures  and  personal 
acknowledgments are not transferred into this study. The chapters are newly written around

2. Data and decision-making in retail
2.1 What the retailer needs to know
A replenishment decision draws on several kinds of information. Sales history helps estimate 
what customers may buy. The stock balance describes what can be sold now. Expected 
deliveries show what may become available later. Supplier conditions determine what can be 
ordered and when it may arrive. Spending authority determines which feasible purchases can 
be released. These inputs answer different questions, and they can come from different parts 
of the organisation. Combining them into a usable decision is therefore an information-
management task as well as a forecasting task.
The stock figure illustrates why the meaning of a field matters. Stock on the premises may 
include goods that are already reserved, damaged or close to expiry. An incoming purchase 
order may describe goods that have been requested rather than goods that the supplier has 
confirmed. A manager may know the difference from working with the store, while a system 
receives only a number. The system needs a clear definition of what that number represents. 
Otherwise, arithmetic performed correctly on the wrong interpretation can produce a poor 
recommendation.
Wang and Strong (1996) argue that data quality extends beyond accuracy to the qualities that 
make data useful to their consumers. That perspective fits replenishment particularly well. A 
value can be accurate when recorded yet unsuitable for today's order if it refers to an old 
supplier agreement. A quantity can be complete yet difficult to use if its unit is unclear. 
Consistency across fields, availability at the decision time and relevance to the intended 
action all contribute to whether information is useful.
These considerations suggest a practical order of work. The retailer first needs to identify the 
source and meaning of each input, then decide how it will be used in the order process. A 
language-model role can assist with interpreting an incoming message, but it still needs a 
current source and an agreed field definition. The planning tool then needs those fields 
alongside the stock and forecast. The experiment follows this separation so that supplier-
reading  errors  can  be  distinguished  from  the  numerical  consequences  of  an  otherwise 
correctly specified plan.

2.2 Sales history and the limits of observed demand
Recorded sales show what a retailer sold, rather than every purchase customers might have 
wanted to make. When a shelf is empty, some customers may leave without buying the 
product. Others may switch to another product, defer their purchase or shop elsewhere. The 
sales record alone does not identify how many of these events occurred. This difference 
between sales and demand is especially relevant when the forecast is used to decide how 
much stock to carry in the next period.
An illustrative example makes the problem clear. Suppose a product sells ten units before 
running out at midday. The recorded daily total is ten. It does not follow that ten units would 
have met the full day's demand. However, it also does not follow that the missing demand 
was another ten units. The time of the stockout gives useful information about availability, 
but the quantity that customers would have purchased remains uncertain. A researcher who 
inserts a large estimated addition into the data would be making a modelling assumption 
rather than observing those purchases.
Nahmias (1994) discusses demand estimation in settings where unavailable stock causes lost 
sales.  This  literature  explains  why  retail  forecasts  need  careful  interpretation  when 
availability constrains the observations. The problem is not resolved simply by choosing a 
more complex prediction model. The target itself may contain a limitation. A model can learn 
the recorded sales pattern accurately and still provide an incomplete estimate of demand if 
frequent stockouts have shaped that pattern.
FreshRetailNet-50K was released specifically to support research into this issue. Its dataset 
card describes normalized daily and hourly sales, stockout-status information and contextual 
variables for fresh retail (Dingdong-Inc., 2025). Wang et al. (2025) describe the source as a 
stockout-annotated censored-demand dataset. That description makes the release useful for 
studying the relationship between observed transactions and availability. It also cautions 
against treating recorded transactions as an unrestricted measure of customer interest.
The present study uses released sales as its demand proxy and does not estimate unrecorded 
purchases. This choice keeps the experiment focused on replenishment coordination and 
avoids adding another inferred mechanism to the system. The resulting fulfilment measure 
refers to the demand world defined in the simulator. It should be read as the share of that 
declared demand that the policy satisfied. It is not an estimate of either retailer's actual

Page 15 of 70
historical service level, and the dissertation does not describe the study as recovering true 
latent demand.
2.3 Inventory information, timing and visibility
Inventory records carry their own uncertainties in a live business. Goods can be misplaced, 
damaged,  sold  through  an  unrecorded  process  or  received  under  an  incorrect  code. 
DeHoratius and Raman (2008) provide an empirical basis for treating inventory-record 
inaccuracy as an operational concern. Kang and Gershwin (2005) also connect information 
inaccuracies with stock loss and stockouts. These studies support a general point: the quality 
of the stock information can influence performance even when the ordering rule itself is 
sensible.
The current experiment deliberately provides a consistent simulated physical ledger. It does 
not introduce corrupt inventory fields or attempt inventory-record repairs. The inventory 
literature is used to explain why clear information definitions matter, rather than to claim that 
this study tested those faults. Keeping that boundary visible prevents the dissertation from 
presenting an untested recovery capability as part of the new system. It also makes the 
research question easier to follow: the study examines what happens when a language-model 
workflow uses a known stock state and current supplier information.
Timing remains important even when the ledger is consistent. On-hand stock and stock on 
order are different. A delivery due after a promotion cannot meet sales that occur during the 
promotion. An order with a longer lead time may require a different stock target from an 
order that can arrive tomorrow. The planning process needs to use both quantity and timing. 
Considering all incoming stock as immediately available would make the decision appear 
better protected than it actually is.
The experiment records future deliveries separately from goods that have arrived. It also 
models delivery cancellations, partial fulfilment and occasional extra-day delays. These are 
disclosed simulation assumptions rather than observed supplier events. The same underlying 
delivery opportunities are used for matched policies. This allows the study to examine how 
different order decisions respond to equivalent supply conditions, while preserving a clear 
account of which parts of the operating environment came from the datasets and which parts 
were constructed for evaluation.

2.4 Forecasting as support for a decision
A sales forecast is useful because future demand is uncertain at the time the order is placed. 
Historical  patterns  can provide  information about  recent  activity,  weekly  variation  and 
changes in volume. The forecast does not promise that tomorrow will exactly repeat the past. 
It gives the ordering process a consistent estimate against which to plan. The choice of 
forecasting model should therefore reflect both its predictive purpose and the decision 
process that will consume its output.
The M5 competition demonstrates the importance of evaluating forecasting methods on a 
substantial retail benchmark. Makridakis et al. (2022) discuss the competition's results and 
the questions they raise for practical forecasting. For this dissertation, M5 contributes an 
established  retail-data  setting  rather  than  a  claim  that  the  experiment  reproduces  the 
competition leaderboard. The study uses a small selected panel and a common forecasting 
method for all ordering policies. Its principal comparison concerns replenishment workflows, 
not a contest among many forecasting families.
Retail sales can be uneven. A product may sell on only some days, or a small number of large 
sales may dominate its recent history. Croston (1972) and Syntetos and Boylan (2005) show 
why intermittent-demand forecasting needs particular care. These contributions explain a 
difficulty that managers recognise immediately: a quiet sequence of days can coexist with 
occasional demand that is operationally important. A single average can hide that pattern. At 
the same time, a highly variable forecast may encourage an ordering process to hold more 
stock than it can reasonably sell.
The study uses LightGBM, a numerical machine-learning forecasting family, to provide the 
common sales estimate. The model uses historical sales patterns and calendar information 
available at the decision origin. It is fitted afresh for each dataset and each of the two weekly 
evaluation origins. The language model does not produce these forecasts. Keeping the 
forecasting  family  the  same  across  policies  means  that  a  difference  in  replenishment 
performance cannot be explained by one policy receiving a different forecasting model.
Forecasting accuracy still matters as background context. A replenishment workflow cannot 
assume that an uncertain forecast has become a known future quantity simply because it 
comes from machine learning. Gneiting and Raftery (2007) highlight the importance of 
evaluating predictions in relation to their intended probabilistic meaning. The current study 
does not claim to estimate a fully calibrated demand distribution. It uses a shared point

Page 17 of 70
forecast and a training-derived uncertainty allowance within the disclosed planning rules. The 
forecasting context is retained so that inventory outcomes can be interpreted alongside the 
uncertainty of the common sales estimate.
2.5 Delivery delays, information changes and allocation
Lead time affects how long a store must rely on stock it already holds. If a delivery takes 
longer, an order placed today cannot immediately remove today's shortage. The retailer may 
need to hold a larger buffer, order earlier or accept that part of the demand cannot be met by 
that delivery. Clark and Scarf (1960) establish the importance of inventory-policy structure in 
systems  with  multiple  stages.  Although  the  present  experiment  is  simpler  than  a  full 
distribution network, the underlying lesson remains useful: inventory has to be planned in 
relation to when and where it becomes available.
Supplier conditions can also change within an evaluation period. An increased carton size 
changes the quantities a retailer is permitted to buy. A higher minimum order can make a 
small top-up unavailable. A reduced dispatch limit can require more careful allocation across 
products. These changes need to be communicated to the planner in a usable form. They 
provide a natural setting for the supplier-reading role, because the practical challenge is to 
recognise the current rule and preserve its meaning while expressing it as a field.
Lee et al. (1997) and Chen et al. (2000) discuss how information, forecasting and lead times 
can contribute to order variation through a supply chain. Their research provides a reason to 
look beyond the accuracy of a single forecast. Ordering behaviour can introduce additional 
variation when information is interpreted or acted on differently at successive points. The 
current dissertation does not claim to measure a multi-stage bullwhip effect. It uses this 
literature to motivate consistent information and clearly specified policy responses within one 
replenishment workflow.
Allocation creates a related management choice. A fixed supplier limit can make several 
individually  reasonable  orders  jointly  impossible.  Proportionate  allocation  offers  an 
understandable rule, but it does not always favour the products for which stock is most 
urgently needed. A constrained planner can consider different shortage priorities while 
keeping the same hard spending and supplier limits. The contrast between these approaches is 
part of the experiment. It helps explain why an agent-and-tool package may improve on 
conventional ordering even when the language model does not improve on the numerical 
planner alone.

2.6 Perishability changes the meaning of available stock
Fresh retail adds time to the definition of usable inventory. Two stores can hold the same 
quantity of a product yet face different ordering needs if one holds recently delivered stock 
and the other holds stock that will expire tonight. The older stock may satisfy today's demand 
while offering little protection for a delivery that arrives two days later. A replenishment 
system that considers only the total quantity can overlook this difference and underestimate 
the need for a future delivery.
Nahmias  (1982)  reviews  the  inventory  problems  created  by  perishability.  The  central 
business issue is that unsold stock can lose its ability to generate a sale. Carrying more 
inventory therefore has a consequence beyond the ordinary financing and storage burden. The 
retailer may eventually dispose of it. This gives managers a reason to assess availability and 
expiry  together.  A  policy  that  raises  service  can  still  be  unattractive  if  the  additional 
availability comes with an unacceptable amount of waste.
The physical simulator uses first-in, first-out consumption for RetailNet. Older usable batches 
are consumed before newer ones, and remaining stock reaches the end of its hypothetical 
three-day life according to its batch age. This is a defined way of making age visible to the 
ordering tool. It does not establish the actual shelf life of the products in the released dataset, 
and it does not measure observed retailer disposal. The expiry results concern the modelled 
stock process used to evaluate the policies.
For M5, the simulator treats stock as nonperishable within the seven-day episode. This makes 
the comparison different from RetailNet even when the same broad ordering workflow is 
used. The difference is intentional and disclosed. It gives the dissertation a way to examine 
the  role  of  an  availability–expiry  trade-off,  while  keeping  the  datasets'  quantity  scales 
separate. A lower expiry share in one policy is consequently a finding about the declared 
fresh-stock simulation, rather than evidence of an environmental saving achieved by either 
source retailer.
2.7 Separating observations, predictions and assumptions
Research on a business system becomes harder to interpret when every value is described 
simply as data. The sales histories in this study are released observations. The future sales 
estimates are model outputs based on earlier observations. The stock, supplier capacities and 
purchase events are constructed within a simulator. The current supplier messages describe

Page 19 of 70
that constructed environment. These elements can all inform a decision, but they support 
different kinds of conclusions.
This separation matters when the results are communicated. A forecast line in a graph should 
not appear to be an observed future quantity. A supplier dispatch limit should not appear to 
have been reported by the dataset publisher when it was created for the experiment. An 
operating-cost reduction should not be presented as a change in a retailer's audited accounts. 
Labelling each element helps a business reader distinguish what the study measured from the 
assumptions that made the measurement possible.

The supplier messages are controlled examples generated from current simulated contract

arrangement is particularly useful for examining the effect of supplier reading without 
allowing an incorrect field to become a silently accepted business rule.
2.8 What the performance measures mean to a manager
Customer-demand fulfilment is the most direct service measure in the study. It is the share of 
simulated demand that the policy can satisfy from usable stock. A higher percentage means 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 20 of 70
that more of the declared demand was served. This definition makes the measure easy to 
connect to availability, while retaining its simulation boundary. It does not claim to measure 
customer satisfaction, brand loyalty or store visits, none of which are observed in the 
experiment.
The inventory operating-cost index combines holding stock, leaving demand unfulfilled and 
allowing stock to expire. It provides a common assessment rule within each dataset. The 
coefficients are explicit experimental choices, rather than estimates of the retailers' actual 
margins or disposal charges. Purchase spend is reported separately. This keeps the primary 
measure focused on the inventory consequences and avoids adding both acquisition and 
expiry in a way that would count the same goods twice without a complete commercial 
accounting model.
The cost index and service measure should be read together. A lower cost can be achieved by 
buying less, which may also reduce service. A higher service rate may require more stock, 
which can increase holding or expiry. Looking at only one outcome would conceal part of the 
trade-off. RetailNet  expiry is  therefore  reported separately, and the  study records held 
decisions and committed-action constraint violations as additional descriptions of how the 
process operated.
The two reference policies give these measures their interpretation. Against conventional 
ordering,  an  improvement  describes  the  complete  agent-and-tool  package.  Against  the 
structured-data planner, a difference concerns the additional interpretation and coordination 
layer within that package. The research does not infer a universal benefit from either 
comparison. It asks what happened in the selected retail panels under a common declared 
evaluation  world,  then  uses  that  evidence  to  identify  the  most  credible  role  for  local 
language-model assistance.

3. Language models as replenishment partners
3.1 What a language model brings to the process
A language model is useful here because replenishment information can be expressed in 
words. It can connect different ways of describing a rule to a consistent business meaning. A 
statement that a supplier ships cartons of six and a table headed carton size with the value six 
describe  the  same  ordering  condition.  Recognising  that  relationship  is  distinct  from 
calculating the quantity the store should buy. The model's potential contribution begins with 
interpretation, while the ordering calculation still depends on the stock, forecast and physical 
constraints.
The  model  produces  its  response  using  patterns  learned  during  earlier  training.  In  an 
instruction-following setting, those patterns are guided by the task description and the 
information supplied for the current decision. This can support flexible handling of ordinary 
language and tabular text. It does not give the model an automatic understanding of a 
retailer's internal conventions. A term such as available stock may have a particular meaning 
in one business and a different meaning in another. The task needs to state what the model is 
expected to identify or decide.
Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct is the pretrained model family used in the experiment. The model 
card describes an instruction-tuned language model with support for structured inputs and 
outputs (Qwen Team, 2024). The  study uses the local  Ollama tag qwen2.5:1.5b, in a 
compressed representation suitable for CPU inference. Its size is relevant to the research 
because it allows a substantial number of fresh calls within the available computing budget. 
The experiment examines this particular small model and configuration rather than treating 
its performance as representative of every language model.
A model card establishes what software is being used and how its publisher describes it. It 
does not establish the model's accuracy on the retailer's own messages. The study therefore 
evaluates the actual responses returned for the current supplier text and business context. It 
records those responses rather than relying on the model's general reputation. That distinction 
is useful to managers: choosing an established model family can be a reasonable starting 
point, but a credible adoption decision still needs evidence from the responsibilities assigned 
to it.

3.2 From a conversational assistant to a bounded agent role
The term agent can describe systems with very different degrees of freedom. Some can plan 
long sequences of actions, select among many tools and revise their approach after receiving 
feedback. Others perform a small role in a predefined workflow. The system studied in this 
dissertation belongs to the latter group. It uses three task-specific language-model calls within 
a fixed sequence. The agent roles do not independently change the retailer's budget, invent a 
supplier agreement or retrain the forecasting model.
The first role reads the supplier information. The second requests an approved planning tool 
and selects a permitted service priority. The third reviews the outcome of independent checks 
and gives an approval or a hold. These responsibilities are deliberately separated so that their 
behaviour can be examined. Each role has its own instructions and current inputs, but all use 
the  same  underlying  Qwen  model.  The  system  is  consequently  a  multiple-role  agent 
workflow implemented through one model family, rather than a panel of independently 
trained specialists.
Yao et al. (2023) discuss the relationship between language-model reasoning and acting 
through  external  tools.  Their  ReAct  work  provides  a  useful  conceptual  reference  for 
connecting a language model to actions whose consequences can be inspected. The present 
system is not a reproduction of the full ReAct method. It adopts the general idea of giving a 
model a defined interaction with tools, while limiting the sequence and available responses to 
suit a replenishment experiment that can be audited.
This limited arrangement is appropriate to the business question. Replenishment involves 
money, physical stock and service expectations, so flexibility needs to be connected to an 
explicit purpose. The model can interpret a message and route a request, while a numerical 
tool calculates a feasible plan. The process can then check whether the proposed order 
respects the current rules. Each responsibility remains visible. That makes it  easier to 
investigate an error and explain the resulting business action than it would be in a system that 
allows every component to improvise its own policy.

3.3 Why numerical planning remains a separate responsibility
The ordering tool performs a different kind of work from the language model. It combines the 
forecast with on-hand stock, incoming deliveries and current ordering conditions. It considers 
carton sizes, minimum orders, supplier capacity and the daily budget. These requirements 
need to hold jointly across the portfolio. A plan that is suitable for one product may become 
unsuitable when considered with the other products competing for the same supplier capacity. 
Consistent arithmetic and constraint checking are therefore essential parts of the process.
Language models can assist with optimisation problems by connecting business questions to 
suitable numerical machinery. Li et al. (2023) explore this relationship in a supply-chain 
context. The useful distinction is between interpreting a request and solving the underlying 
constrained problem. A model  can help express  what the  business wants  or select  an 
approved  computation,  while  a  solver  applies  the  rules  to  the  actual  quantities.  The 
experiment  follows  this  division  rather  than  asking  the  model  to  produce  thirty  order 
quantities directly from prose.
The separation also supports evaluation. If the agent-assisted system and the structured-data 
planner use the same numerical tool, differences can be examined in relation to the language-
model layer. If they instead used different tools and forecasts, a performance gap would be 
harder to interpret. It could reflect any of those changes. Keeping the shared numerical 
capability visible allows the study to ask a more precise question about the additional roles 
than a comparison of two entirely different systems would permit.
This does not make the numerical planner infallible. It operates on a forecast, a defined cost 
preference and a limited planning horizon. Its assumptions may be unsuitable for a particular 
retailer. A feasible plan can still produce a shortage because demand is uncertain or a delivery 
is delayed. The important point is that the numerical component has an explicit responsibility 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 24 of 70
and can be examined on that basis. The language model's interpretation, the planner's quantity 
decision and the physical outcome are retained as related but separately inspectable stages.
3.4 The supplier-reading role
The supplier-reading role has a concrete task: extract the current ordering conditions without 
changing their meaning. For each of the three synthetic supplier groups, it identifies carton 
size, minimum order, delivery days and dispatch capacity. It also extracts the current daily 
budget. This gives thirteen numerical fields for each supplier-reading call. Supplier identity 
and coverage matter as well, because a correct value assigned to the wrong supplier would 
still provide the planner with an incorrect rule.
The messages use three controlled formats. The prose format describes the conditions in a 
consistent sequence. The memo format changes the order in which quantities appear. The 
table format places the fields under headings. These formats test whether the model connects 
a number to its meaning rather than simply copying numbers in the order they appear. A 
capacity of ninety-seven units, for example, needs to remain a dispatch limit even when it is 
the first number in a sentence that later mentions a carton of three.
The extraction is checked against the authenticated current contract fields represented by the 
message. A disagreement produces a hold rather than a corrected response or a substituted 
conventional order. This gives the experimental workflow a clear treatment of failure. The 
model's answer is preserved, its accuracy is scored and the proposal is not released through 
an alternative policy. The system does not quietly remove an interpretation error from the 
evidence by replacing it with a successful result from another component.
The controlled source has limits. It does not contain ambiguous attachments, conflicting 
negotiation histories or unrestricted commercial legal language. A good extraction result 
therefore supports the task actually tested. It gives a reason to investigate a language interface 
for supplier rules, but it does not establish that every supplier communication can be handled 
without  review.  The  dissertation  retains  that  distinction  when  interpreting  the  reading 
accuracy. Managers should ask whether their own document variation is similar to, narrower 
than or broader than the variation represented by this experimental corpus.
3.5 The coordination role and business priorities
The coordination role connects a current business situation to the approved planning process. 
It  is  instructed  to  request  the  constrained-order  tool.  For  routine  trading  and  supplier 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 25 of 70
disruption,  the  expected  priority  is  balanced.  For  an  active  announced  promotion,  the 
expected  priority  gives  additional  weight  to  service.  This  is  a  defined  business-policy 
mapping within the experiment. The language model does not choose a new objective for the 
retailer or assign its own commercial value to every customer shortage.
The role receives the current scenario, whether a promotion is active, aggregate stock, the 
current forecast and the verified supplier information. These inputs describe the decision as it 
appears to the coordination role. They do not include realised future demand. The tool then 
uses the chosen priority with the full portfolio state to calculate quantities. This arrangement 
allows a routing or priority-selection error to affect the proposed plan in a visible way, rather 
than being reduced to an isolated classification score without an operational consequence.
An important distinction arises between a permitted response and the correct policy response. 
The role can select a priority that is recognised by the system yet inconsistent with the 
instruction for that situation. A valid field therefore does not guarantee a correct choice. The 
order may still respect the supplier and spending limits, so a physical constraint check alone 
will not reveal the semantic error. Measuring priority selection separately is necessary to 
understand how the model followed the business instruction.
This  is  a  useful  management  lesson  even  before  considering  the  empirical  findings. 
Organisations often combine policy statements with numerical systems. A phrase such as 
protect availability during the promotion needs a defined relationship with the planning 
mode. If that relationship is left unclear, a system can produce technically acceptable actions 
that differ from the intended business response. The experiment makes the expected mapping 
explicit and retains the model's actual choice, allowing the study to assess interpretation of 
policy as well as interpretation of supplier numbers.
3.6 Review and the authority to execute
The review role receives the results of independent checks on the proposal. It considers 
whether source verification passed, the approved tool was requested, the solver produced a 
feasible proposal and the proposed quantities passed the listed action checks. It then returns 
an approval or a hold. This makes review a visible stage in the workflow. The model is asked 
to apply a stated release rule, rather than infer permission from the tone of a supplier message 
or the confidence of another agent role.
The final execution gate remains deterministic. The gate only permits release when the 
required conditions pass and the review role approves. This means the language model cannot 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 26 of 70
override a failed budget or supplier check. It also means that an approval from the model is 
not itself the complete authority to buy. The release process combines the model's review 
with conditions imposed outside the model. This is how the system maintains the distinction 
between proposing an action and being permitted to execute it.
Parasuraman et al. (2000) describe  different  functions and levels  of automation. Their 
framework is helpful because a retailer can automate information processing without granting 
unrestricted authority over implementation. The supplier-reading role, the planning tool and 
the release gate have different functions in that arrangement. Parasuraman and Riley (1997) 
also draw attention to how people use or misuse automation. These contributions support a 
process view in which the manager needs to understand what each automated component is 
authorised to do.
The study does not claim that review by a language model is a substitute for a retailer's 
governance. The independent numerical checks already perform substantial work. In the 
completed main experiment, the review task largely involved proposals whose checks passed. 
The absence of false approvals in those cases does not establish how well the review role 
would detect a broad range of deliberately unsafe plans. Those conditions were outside the 
requested clean study. The empirical interpretation therefore centres on the observed guarded 
workflow rather than an unrestricted promise of model judgement.
3.7 Instructions, development and stable evaluation
The task instructions supplied to a language model are often called prompts. In this study, 
they state the responsibility of each role and the permitted response format. The supplier role 
is told how to connect each number to its field. The coordinator is told how to request the 
approved tool and choose the business priority. The review role is told when to approve and 
when to hold. These instructions are part of the experimental system because changing them 
can change the behaviour being evaluated.
Development checks were conducted before the protocol was frozen. The first round exposed 
a field-swapping error and a review error. Small worked examples were then added to clarify 
the intended reading and review rules. This was task configuration, not training of the 
model's weights. The development records were retained separately, and they were not 
counted as primary evaluation results. This distinction gives the researcher a legitimate 
opportunity to make the system functional while preserving an honest account of what was 
discovered during development.

Page 27 of 70
Once the protocol was frozen, the study used the same instructions, examples, model settings 
and evaluated policy code throughout the complete grid. It did not revise the prompts after 
examining the primary outcomes. There were no retries of a failed answer, no cached-
response substitution and no evaluation-time repair. Fresh native Ollama responses were 
saved for every role call. That procedure makes the primary findings evidence about the 
frozen  system  rather than evidence  about  an  evolving  collection of responses  selected 
because they happened to work.
Stable  evaluation  is  especially  important  for  a  system  with  several  roles.  If  a  poor 
coordination result led to a new instruction halfway through the study, earlier and later seeds 
would no longer evaluate the same process. A larger call count would then provide a 
misleading appearance of consistency. Hevner et al. (2004) emphasise evaluation within 
design-science research. The present study responds by separating development from the 
registered evaluation and by preserving both the unsuccessful development cases and the 
complete primary decision records.

The structured-data planner is the main test of the language-model layer. It receives the 
current rules in organised fields and applies the same numerical planning tool, stock-age 
projection and scenario-priority mapping used by the agent workflow. Its inputs describe an 
idealised information state. This makes it a stronger comparison than a simple ordering rule, 
because it removes the need for the additional interpretation and coordination calls while 
retaining the planning capability.

4. Research framework and experiment design
4.1 An experiment that separates the package from its parts
The  research  uses  a  matched  simulation  experiment  to  evaluate  a  newly  defined 
replenishment workflow. Historical retail sales provide the quantity patterns. A disclosed 
physical simulator then represents stock, purchase decisions, incoming deliveries and expiry. 
Three policies face the same sales-derived demand and supply opportunities within each 
matched case. Their decisions can therefore be compared without allowing one policy to 
benefit from a more favourable demand realization or an easier supplier event simply because 
it was evaluated in a different run.
The  experimental  design  answers  the  three  research  questions  at  different  levels.  The 
comparison between conventional ordering and the agent-assisted workflow addresses the 
performance of the complete package. The comparison between the structured-data planner 
and the agents addresses the incremental language-model layer. Role-level scores address 
supplier interpretation, priority selection and review behaviour. This organisation makes a 
positive package result useful without requiring the dissertation to assume that the language 
model caused the entire improvement.
The full registered grid includes two datasets, two weekly origins per dataset, three operating 
scenarios, thirty simulation seeds and three policies. This gives 1,080 policy episodes. Each 
episode lasts seven days and contains all thirty selected series for its dataset. The full grid 
therefore contains 7,560 portfolio decision days and 226,800 series-days. The agent policy 
occupies 2,520 portfolio days and makes three fresh language-model calls on every one of 
those days. Its 7,560 model calls are separate from the total policy decision count, despite the 
two totals having the same numerical value.
The design was frozen before primary execution. The instructions, worked examples, policy 
code, selected data, forecasts, model settings and completion target were recorded with file 
hashes. The evaluation ran to completion rather than stopping after favourable seed rounds. 
Development records remain separate, and earlier experiments are not pooled into the current 
findings. This dissertation uses the complete new study as its quantitative foundation, with 
the earlier project versions retained only as historical records in the repository.

For RetailNet, the first week is a rolling holdout from the released training file, while the

week using only earlier observations. The earlier week's recorded sales can consequently be 
part of the second model's historical training information once that week has occurred. They 
are never supplied to the first model as future inputs. The sequence reflects a normal 
distinction between information known at one decision origin and information that becomes 
known at the next.
The historical periods have also been used elsewhere in the wider dissertation project. Fresh 
execution does not make them previously unseen research holdouts. The current study 
protects forecasting inputs from future observations and freezes the new evaluation system 
before its primary run, but it does not claim a completely untouched research environment. 
This distinction is disclosed because protection against forecast leakage and freedom from all 
prior researcher familiarity are different standards.
The protocol records both historical evaluation dates and current execution timestamps. The 
main study completed on 9 October 2026, with 2.66 hours of live evaluation. Development, 
data preparation, fresh forecast fitting and reporting were additional activities within the 
requested time budget. The historical dates describe the retail observations used in the 
experiment; the execution date describes when the local system was assessed. Keeping those 
two timelines separate prevents the simulator from being presented as a live store deployment 
during either historical period.
4.4 One forecasting family shared by every policy
The  study  uses  one  numerical  forecasting  family:  LightGBM,  the  tree-based  method 
described by Ke et al. (2017). A separate fit is produced for each dataset and weekly origin, 
giving four primary fitted forecast models. These are four fits of the same family, not four 
competing methods. The approach keeps the replenishment comparisons focused. All three 
policies within a matched case use the same predictions, so a performance gap cannot be 
attributed to one policy having access to a different forecaster or additional future-sales 
information.
The model uses earlier sales at several lags, recent averages and variability, the series 
identifier and calendar information. The training window is limited to at most one year before 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 33 of 70
an origin. RetailNet has a shorter available history, so its fitted model uses the eligible earlier 
observations that exist in that release. A minimum history is required to form the lag features. 
The model predicts recursively: after making a prediction, that predicted quantity helps form 
the next step's input, rather than replacing it with the actual evaluation sale.
The forecast extends fourteen days from each origin so that delivery-aware planning can use 
estimates beyond the current day. Only the declared seven-day episode is used for primary 
outcome measurement. This difference between the forecast extension and the evaluation 
horizon is practical: an order placed near the end of a week may arrive after the immediate 
sales day, so the planner needs a consistent future estimate for its target. The study does not 
report the extra forecast days as additional evaluated retail observations.
An uncertainty allowance is derived from historical weekly sales changes. It is based on 
training information rather than the forecast errors observed during evaluation. The ordering 
tools use that allowance with the forecast when forming a stock target. The procedure 
supplies a consistent buffer to the policies, but it is not described as a fully calibrated 
probability forecast. Forecast-context measures are retained in an appendix, where they help 
the reader understand the uncertainty behind the common numerical input without turning the 
dissertation into a forecasting-model competition.

4.5 The three ordering policies
The conventional policy uses an order-up-to approach. It estimates how much stock is needed 
over  the  relevant  delivery  period,  accounts  for  current  stock  and  qualifying  incoming 
deliveries, and rounds quantities to the permitted cartons. If supplier or spending limits 
prevent all desired orders, it allocates capacity proportionately and adjusts the final lines to 
respect the rules. The policy is understandable and provides a consistent reference. It uses 
aggregate inventory position rather than the detailed age projection used by the planning tool.
The structured-data planner uses current authenticated rules with a constrained-order tool. It 
projects usable stock and expected consumption, then allocates proposed orders under the 
supplier and budget limits. For RetailNet, it considers batch age and the stock expected to 
remain usable when deliveries arrive. It also applies the declared promotion-priority rule 
directly. This policy provides the strong reference for the agent system because the numerical 
tool, age projection and intended priority mapping are shared.
The agent policy adds the supplier reader, planning coordinator and review role to that same 
numerical  capability.  The  reader  extracts  current  terms  from  a  message.  Exact  source 
agreement is required before planning can proceed. The coordinator requests the tool and

Page 35 of 70
selects a permitted priority. The tool calculates the proposed order vector for the thirty-series 
portfolio. The review role assesses the independent checks, and the execution gate determines 
whether the proposal is permitted. A failed condition leads to a hold, with the original model 
outputs preserved.
The comparisons have different purposes. Conventional ordering versus agents measures a 
complete workflow change, including the effect of the numerical tool and its treatment of 
stock. The structured-data planner versus agents asks what changes when the interpretation 
and coordination roles are added to that capability. The latter is an idealised comparison 
because it starts with already organised information. The study does not assign a financial 
saving to the staff work that might be needed to produce those inputs in an actual retailer.

4.6 Operating situations with clear business meanings
Routine trading uses the current supplier rules without an announced disruption or promotion. 
Daily budgets and supplier capacities vary within a small declared range around training-

set of related operating worlds rather than thirty exact copies of one fixed capacity condition. 
It also provides different numerical values for the supplier reader to extract within the 
controlled message formats.

how the policies act under changed rules, rather than testing which policy can infer an

4.7 The physical inventory process
Each matched episode begins with stock derived from the preceding fifty-six days of sales. 
The opening quantity is approximately three historical average days, rounded up, and there is 
no initial delivery pipeline. All policies within a case receive the same opening quantities. 
RetailNet opening stock is distributed across three age classes. This represents an initial 
mixture of fresh and older usable goods rather than assuming that every opening unit has just 
arrived. M5 stock remains usable throughout the weekly episode.
The decision sequence is consistent across days. Deliveries that are due arrive before the 
current decision. The policy then considers the stock state, current forecast and supplier 
conditions. Permitted orders enter the future delivery pipeline according to their lead times 
and supply draws. Simulated sales consume the available stock, and unsatisfied demand is 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 37 of 70
recorded as lost demand. For RetailNet, older batches are consumed first and any remaining 
units at the end of their usable life expire after that day's sales opportunity.
The simulator keeps purchase, receipt, sale, expiry, closing-stock and pipeline quantities in a 
physical ledger. Accepted quantities may arrive only partly filled or after an extra day, while 
cancelled quantities do not become stock. This makes it possible to check where every unit in 
the modelled process has gone. It also prevents an order from being treated as immediately 
available simply because it has been placed. Closing pipeline is recorded separately from the 
stock that can serve demand on the final day.
The weekly horizon creates a limitation that is retained in interpretation. Some closing stock 
and deliveries may have value beyond the measured episode. The experiment records those 
balances but does not simulate a long-run operating history or add an observed commercial 
salvage value. As a result, the operating-cost measure is useful for the declared comparison, 
while a retailer's longer-term profit and stock rotation would require a longer evaluation and 
more complete business accounting.
4.8 Matched demand and supplier opportunities
The thirty simulation seeds generate variation around the recorded sales proxy and supplier 
conditions. The demand proxy is multiplied by a modest mean-one random factor, with the 
additional announced uplift in the promotion case. This retains the source's daily pattern 
while representing uncertainty within the defined world. The factor is not a correction for 
naturally censored demand. It does not estimate how many customers were turned away by 
actual stockouts in the original retailer.
Supplier opportunities include a two per cent cancellation probability, a ten per cent chance 
of partial fulfilment at eighty per cent of the committed quantity, and a ten per cent chance of 
an extra delivery day. These probabilities are experimental assumptions. The draws are keyed 
to the seed, decision day and series. A policy that orders more often therefore does not 
change the sequence of random opportunities facing the other policies simply because it 
consumes a different number of random draws.
This matching is important to the interpretation of a cost or service difference. If one policy 
received a late delivery while another received an on-time delivery only because their 
random-number sequences had drifted apart, the comparison would mix policy behaviour 
with an accidental simulation difference. The opportunity-keyed approach gives the same 
relevant supply draw to equivalent order opportunities. Policies can still experience different 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 38 of 70
realised quantities because their committed orders differ, which is part of the consequence of 
the policy decision itself.
The seed is the unit used for the paired uncertainty analysis. Days and products within an 
episode are related through stock, capacity and the delivery pipeline. They are not treated as 
independent observations. Both weekly origins are aggregated within each seed before the 
comparison, and the three scenarios remain visible in the separate graphs. This lets the study 
use a substantial number of recorded series-days without presenting that total as a larger 
number of independent retailers or independent language-model experiments.
4.9 Model, training and computing environment
The local language model is pretrained Qwen2.5 1.5B, served through the local Ollama 
interface (Ollama, 2026) on a Linux cloud computer. The machine has four allocated CPU 
cores and 32 GiB of memory. No GPU was used for inference. The model's weights were 
already trained by its publisher. They were not trained or fine-tuned on M5 or FreshRetailNet 
in this project. The retailer-specific work consisted of role instructions, current inputs and the 
connection to the numerical tool and release checks.
LightGBM is the component fitted to the retail histories. Its four primary fits were trained 
locally on that same cloud environment, with each fit restricted to observations preceding its 
weekly origin. It used one numerical fitting thread. The language-model calls used three CPU 
threads and a working context of 4,096 tokens. The model was configured at temperature 
zero with a fixed generation seed and bounded structured responses. These settings make the 
evaluated configuration explicit; they do not imply that every future model or computer will 
reproduce identical latency or behaviour.
The distinction between training and inference is central to reporting this environment 
accurately. Training adjusts model parameters using data. Inference uses existing parameters 
to produce a response for a current input. The Qwen model performed inference in this 
experiment. The LightGBM forecasts were fitted using past retail sales. Adding worked 
prompt examples before the evaluation freeze did not fine-tune Qwen's weights. Calling the 
whole system a model trained on these datasets would obscure these different activities.
The environment table summarises the practical setting, while the appendix records the 
detailed software and generation settings. The CPU-only arrangement allowed all fresh 
primary calls to finish within the study budget. It does not provide a production cost estimate. 
Cloud charges, integration work, employee review and system support were not monetised.

4.10 Measures, aggregation and uncertainty
The primary service measure is demand fulfilment: sales served by usable inventory divided 
by the declared demand quantity. The primary economic measure is an inventory operating-
cost index containing holding stock, a penalty for unmet demand and expired units. The index 
uses the same coefficients across matched policies. It is divided by demand when presenting 
comparable policy outcomes within a dataset. Purchase spend remains a separately recorded 
measure, so the dissertation does not describe the index as total procurement cost or real 
profit.
RetailNet expiry is expressed as expired quantity relative to opening inventory plus receipts. 
When the two weekly episodes are aggregated within a seed, the opening inventory from both 
resets is included. The measure therefore reflects the declared input stock for the measured 
episodes.  M5  has  no  expiry  within  the  modelled  weekly  horizon.  Held  decisions  and 
committed-action constraint violations are counted separately. A permitted zero order is a 
valid decision with no purchase; a hold is a decision that the workflow did not permit to 
execute.
The main summary gives equal weight to the three business scenarios. Within a seed and 
scenario, the two weekly origins are aggregated using their demand and outcome totals.

while leaving the broader transfer question open.
4.11 Traceability and independent checks
The study records every primary model request and raw response, each proposal and release 
check, committed quantities and the series-level physical outcomes. It also saves current 
supplier messages and the authenticated rules that those messages describe. These records 
allow an investigator to connect a reading result to the numerical plan and its inventory 
consequences. A reported outcome is therefore supported by a trace of what the system 
received, chose and executed rather than only by a final summary spreadsheet.
The  completed  evaluation  contains  7,560  unique  native  response  timestamps.  Every

4.12 Interpretation boundaries and responsible reporting
The  experiment  represents  selected  retail  sales  patterns  within  a  constructed  operating 
process. It does not observe actual supplier negotiations, historical inventory balances or 
retailer  profit.  The  English  supplier  messages  are  controlled,  the  product  shelf  life  is 
hypothetical and the demand proxy retains the limitation of recorded sales. These boundaries 
are part of the research design. The dissertation reports them before interpreting the gains, so 
that the reader can distinguish a successful simulated workflow from a proven commercial 
investment.
The model also has a limited responsibility. It interprets supplier fields, selects a planning 
priority and reviews explicit checks. It does not independently forecast all demand or 
compute every purchase quantity. A successful complete workflow should consequently be 
attributed to the combination of roles, numerical tools and release conditions. The structured-
data comparison is retained even when it reduces the apparent novelty of the model's 
contribution. That makes the research more useful for a manager deciding which component 
actually needs to be added to an existing system.
No participant study was conducted. The dissertation therefore does not report employee trust 
scores, customer satisfaction, interview findings or measured reductions in planning labour. It 
discusses those outcomes as questions for later work, informed by automation and retail 
literature. The supplied Gilbert thesis is used for structure rather than as a source of retail 
findings. Its wording and personal statements are not incorporated, and the retail results come 
from the completed experiment's own records.
The two short weekly episodes and one fixed model configuration limit generalisation. A 
longer operating cycle or a different model could change the outcomes. The findings chapter 
therefore reports M5 and RetailNet separately, retaining the distinction between package 
performance and the language-model contribution.

6. Implications and recommendations
6.1 The strongest positive finding is a useful division of work
The study demonstrates that a small local language model can participate in a complete 
replenishment workflow. It read the controlled supplier messages correctly, requested the 
approved numerical tool and operated within a process that kept the actual order limits 
explicit. This is a meaningful result for a manager considering how language capabilities 
might fit into existing retail systems. It shows a practical way to assign the model a role 
without asking it to replace every established forecasting and ordering function.
The large M5 package improvement also shows why the workflow deserves attention. 
Availability was higher and the declared operating-cost measure was lower than under the 
conventional reference. The improvement was maintained across the three simulated business 
situations. For a retailer whose existing process resembles that reference, better projection 
and allocation could be valuable. The relevant investment question is then which changes 
explain the gain. The structured-data result indicates that the numerical planning capability 
should receive substantial attention in that assessment.
RetailNet provides a more restrained positive case. The agent package improved its average 
service and operating-cost measures against conventional ordering, while expiry changed 
differently across scenarios. This makes the result closer to the sort of trade-off a fresh 
retailer  would  need  to  examine.  An  attractive  availability  gain  cannot  be  assessed 
independently of the stock that remains unsold. The common physical ledger and separate 
expiry measure make that trade-off visible, rather than allowing a single cost percentage to 
stand in for every business outcome.
Taken together, the datasets support a practical division of work. The language model can 
interpret  supplier  information;  the  numerical  tool  can  turn  verified  information  into 
constrained quantities; the gate can retain authority over release. The contribution is a 
functioning arrangement among these responsibilities. The experiment does not show that 
adding more conversational roles automatically improves inventory. A retailer can use the 
division as a design option while choosing the smallest set of model responsibilities that adds 
measured value to its own process.

6.2 Why attribution changes the investment decision
Attribution matters because different improvements require different investments. Improving 
a numerical planner may involve better stock projection, capacity allocation or integration 
with incoming deliveries. Adding a language interface may involve document handling, role 
instructions and review of extracted fields. If the retailer attributes the whole package 
improvement to the language model, it may prioritise the visible conversational component 
while  leaving  the  more  consequential  numerical  capability  underdeveloped.  The  strong 
comparison helps avoid that mistake.
In M5, the agents and structured-data planner had essentially the same operational result. In 
RetailNet, the agents were slightly worse than that planner, particularly under promotion. The 
study therefore gives no inventory-performance reason to replace a capable structured-data 
planner with the language-model layer when the information is already available in suitable 
fields. A retailer with well-integrated supplier records can reasonably retain its existing 
structured process while testing language assistance only where the information arrives 
outside that process.
The potential benefit of document interpretation remains commercially relevant. Someone 
has to read a supplier memo, identify the current terms and make those terms usable by the 
planner. A model that carries out that conversion reliably could change the effort required. 
However, the current study supplies the structured reference directly and does not measure 
the human work behind it. An investment case would need to include the time taken to 
prepare fields, the cost of reviewing exceptions and the consequences of errors in the actual 
communication environment.
This gives the dissertation a more specific business conclusion than a general claim that 
artificial intelligence improves replenishment. The planning capability produced the strongest 
measured inventory gain. The model performed a useful interpretation task in the tested 
corpus.  Its  additional  financial  value  remains  to  be  measured  against  the  real  cost  of 
producing and maintaining structured information. These statements can all be true together, 
and distinguishing them helps a manager compare the components on the responsibilities they 
actually perform.
6.3 Fixed business rules and the limits of model discretion
The priority-selection result identifies a responsibility that needs particular care. Reading 
numbers  correctly  did  not  guarantee  that  the  coordination  role  followed  the  expected 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 50 of 70
promotion policy. This matters because a priority can change quantities even when the 
resulting order is feasible. Supplier and budget checks can prevent overspending or an invalid 
carton size, yet they do not establish that the plan reflects the retailer's intended service 
preference. Policy correctness and numerical feasibility therefore need separate treatment.
Where a business rule is already explicit, it may be better implemented directly. If the retailer 
has decided that every active promotion uses a particular planning mode, the system can 
apply that mapping without asking a model to reconsider it on every decision. The structured-
data planner in this experiment did exactly that. This does not mean language assistance has 
no role in recognising an announcement or clarifying its meaning. It means the final mapping 
from a verified event to a firm policy can remain a controlled business rule.
RetailNet makes this point concrete. The model's overall priority score was lowered by its 
promotion decisions, even though routine and supplier-disruption priorities were selected 
correctly. A generally high overall score could have concealed this weakness if the results 
had not separated the conditions. Managers should consequently ask for evaluation on the 
decisions that matter most to their process, including promotion activation and exceptional 
delivery terms, rather than relying on an average across many straightforward routine cases.
The  experiment  does  not  identify  a  single  cause  for  the  dataset  difference  in  priority 
selection. Context values, wording and the small model configuration may all be relevant, but 
they were not independently varied to estimate their effects. A later study could investigate 
those factors. The immediate recommendation is supported by the existing evidence: retain 
firm policy mappings where they are available, and assess model-assisted interpretation on 
the actual events that precede those mappings. This focuses the model on a language task 
while keeping a declared operational preference consistent.
6.4 Designing a pilot around the work that is missing
The next useful pilot would examine the part of the business case that the simulation could 
not measure. The retailer would identify a set of actual incoming supplier communications 
and the process currently used to convert them into planning inputs. A language-assisted 
version could then be compared with that existing process. The comparison would assess 
field accuracy, completion time, review effort and the handling of exceptions. It would also 
retain the same numerical planner so that information-processing value is not mixed with a 
simultaneous planning upgrade.

A shadow phase is appropriate as a first step. The system would prepare interpretations and 
recommendations while the retailer's existing process continued to determine live orders. 
Staff could compare the outputs without granting the new workflow independent purchasing 
authority.  This  would  provide  examples  of  ambiguous  terms,  missing  information  and 
differences in commercial conventions that do not appear in the controlled corpus. The 
proposed phase is a recommendation, not an activity performed in this dissertation.
If the shadow results were satisfactory, a reviewed pilot could permit a defined subset of 
orders with explicit human release. The retailer could choose stable suppliers and products 
with clear ordering conditions, then monitor whether the model changes review workload or 
decision quality. A subsequent phase could consider routine bounded release for cases that 
meet established criteria. Each phase would have its own purpose and completion evidence. 
Movement between phases would depend on measured performance, rather than on the fact 
that a previous stage produced convincing demonstrations.

The pilot should measure the complete information process. A model that extracts quickly but 
creates many ambiguous cases may transfer work to the reviewer rather than remove it. A 
model that handles routine messages accurately may still need expensive support when 
supplier formats change. Conversely, it may reduce repetitive preparation even when the final 
purchase quantities remain identical to those from the current planner. That possibility 
explains why unchanged inventory results do not by themselves settle the business case for a 
language interface.
6.5 Measuring value beyond a simulated cost index
The experiment's operating-cost index gives a declared preference for availability, holding 
and expiry. It is useful for comparing policies under common assumptions. A retailer's 
financial appraisal would need a fuller account. Acquisition prices, margins, fixed ordering 
costs, storage, disposal, markdowns and closing inventory value can all influence whether an 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 52 of 70
order is economically attractive. The coefficients used in the simulator cannot be treated as 
the retailer's measured values for these items.
The model layer introduces additional operating costs. They include computing capacity, 
software integration, monitoring, exception review and the work required to maintain the 
connection between supplier information and the planning system. The study records local 
decision time, but it does not price those activities. A financial case should compare the total 
process cost of the current and proposed arrangements, including the effort saved and the 
effort added. A percentage reduction in the inventory index alone does not establish return on 
investment.
Service can also have business consequences that the simulator does not measure. A fulfilled 
sale may support customer retention or reduce substitution, while an unavailable item may 
have a different consequence depending on the retailer and product. The stockout literature 
explains why these effects are plausible, but this study does not observe them. An operational 
pilot could examine relevant customer and store outcomes with an agreed measurement 
design. The present recommendation is to keep those outcomes distinct from the demand-
fulfilment ratio that the experiment actually calculates.
For fresh retail, the same care applies to environmental claims. The RetailNet expiry measure 
makes wasted modelled stock visible, and its scenario differences are relevant to planning. It 
does not measure kilograms of food waste, disposal practices or emissions. Those quantities 
would require actual product and operational information. The study can motivate including 
waste in an adoption decision without presenting a simulated expiry reduction as an achieved 
sustainability result. This is a more credible starting point for a business case that includes 
both service and resource use.
6.6 Work design, visibility and managerial responsibility
Introducing an agent workflow changes how responsibility is expressed in the process. The 
supplier role produces fields. The planner proposes quantities. The reviewer evaluates checks. 
The gate permits release. A manager needs to understand these responsibilities well enough 
to identify where a failure occurred and which part of the system should be adjusted. Naming 
the roles is useful only if the names correspond to actual recorded behaviour and defined 
authority.
The experiment provides that visibility through saved messages, model outputs, proposals 
and physical outcomes. In a retailer, a similar record could help explain why an order was 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 53 of 70
placed or held. The useful explanation would concern the current rule, selected priority, 
forecast and release checks. It need not depend on a lengthy model-generated account of its 
internal reasoning. Turpin et al. (2023) provide a reason to be cautious about treating a 
language model's explanation as a reliable account of the cause of its answer. Recorded 
inputs and checks offer a more inspectable basis for operational review.
Human review should also have a defined purpose. Asking a person to approve every output 
without a clear reason can create a repetitive task in which genuine exceptions receive little 
attention. Parasuraman and Riley (1997) describe problems of automation use and misuse 
that are relevant to this issue. A retailer can design the review process around the items that 
actually require judgement: unclear supplier terms, unusual priority requests or cases that fall 
outside the approved operating range. The numerical checks can handle the rules that are 
already precise.
This  approach  does  not  claim  that  the  dissertation  measured  staff  trust  or  reviewer 
effectiveness. It uses the literature and the observed role differences to identify a practical 
work-design question. A future pilot would need to test whether the evidence presented to 
staff makes review easier and whether the division of responsibilities remains understandable 
in real operating conditions. The experiment supports a transparent process architecture; the 
organisational effect of using it remains a separate empirical question.
6.7 Computing choices and practical scale
The CPU-only result is relevant because it shows that the complete selected-panel study 
could be executed with a small local model. The configuration did not require a GPU or a 
paid external language-model service. Local execution also made it possible to retain the full 
requests  and  responses  within  the  study  environment.  This  supports  the  feasibility  of 
assessing bounded language roles under a modest computing arrangement, while leaving 
production infrastructure and support requirements outside the demonstrated result.
The observed mean latency is tied to the experimental design. A decision covers thirty series 
through one portfolio request to the numerical tool, rather than making a separate language 
call for every product. The supplier reader handles three group contracts, and the coordinator 
selects among a limited set of priorities. These scope choices explain why the call count 
could remain manageable. The same timing should not be multiplied mechanically to claim a 
capacity for all fifty thousand RetailNet series or a retailer with much longer and more varied 
documents.

A production design might also call the model at different points. Current supplier terms may 
remain  valid  for  many  days,  while  a  new  announcement  may  require  immediate 
interpretation. Choosing when to use the model is therefore part of the operating design. The 
clean experiment made fresh calls every agent day so that the role behaviour was fully 
evaluated and no answer cache concealed mistakes. It did not investigate the business effect 
of reducing calls, changing batch size or reusing verified contract fields. Those are reasonable 
questions for a later, separately evaluated system.
The computing decision should follow the task and the evidence. A larger model may follow 
difficult policies more reliably, but it can add inference time or hardware cost. A simpler role 
may avoid that cost while preserving the interpretation benefit. The current results favour 
testing such choices against the actual responsibility assigned to the model. They do not 
support  selecting  a  larger  system  solely  because  it  offers  more  general  conversational 
capability than the bounded replenishment process requires.
6.8 What the two-dataset study contributes
The two datasets strengthen the analysis by showing both a large package gain and a modest 
one.  M5  makes  the  planning  improvement  visible,  particularly  under  difficult  supplier 
conditions. RetailNet shows a reference policy that is closer to the stronger planner and adds 
a  short-lived-stock  trade-off.  The  language  role  that  read  supplier  fields  performed 
consistently in both, while priority selection did not. This combination gives the discussion 
more substance than a single successful example would provide.
The research also makes a distinction that is useful beyond these panels: successful language 
interpretation  and  better  numerical  replenishment  are  different  claims.  The  model  can 
contribute the former without establishing an advantage in the latter. Keeping the same 
planning tool in the strong reference gives that distinction empirical support. The dissertation 
can consequently describe the tested LLM roles positively while retaining a clear account of 
where the operational improvement came from.
The main remaining limits concern transfer. The messages were controlled, the supplier 
events and costs were synthetic, and each dataset contributed only two weekly episodes. The 
selected panels reflect the declared eligibility and volume-stratification rules. The model 
configuration was fixed and the source periods had been used in earlier project work. These 
boundaries limit a broad claim of superiority, yet they also identify the next evidence that 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 55 of 70
would be valuable: real communication variation, a longer operating horizon and measured 
incremental information-processing cost.
The recommendation is to begin with a capable planner and reliable business definitions, then 
evaluate  language  assistance  where  interpreting  incoming  information  creates  a  real 
workload. Keep supplier and spending limits outside the model's discretion. Apply firm 
business-policy mappings directly when they are already known. Use a staged pilot to 
measure the additional value, including cases where purchase quantities remain unchanged. 
This recommendation follows the strongest observed capabilities and the clearest observed 
weakness of the completed study.

7. Conclusion and future research
7.1 Answering the research questions
This dissertation examined a local language-model workflow for retail replenishment using 
thirty selected M5 series and thirty selected RetailNet series. Three roles read supplier 
information, coordinated an approved planning tool and reviewed the release checks. The 
completed study covered two weekly episodes per dataset, three business situations and thirty 
simulation  seeds.  Its  purpose  was  to  assess  the  whole  workflow  and  distinguish  its 
performance from the contribution of the language-model layer.
The  first  research  question  concerned  the  complete  package.  Relative  to  conventional 
ordering, the agent-and-tool workflow improved simulated fulfilment by 25.28 percentage 
points in M5 and 0.78 points in RetailNet. The paired inventory operating-cost reductions 
were 70.34% and 4.52%, respectively. The improvement was therefore substantial in the 
selected M5 world and modest in the selected fresh-retail world. These are comparative 
simulation findings, rather than observed changes in retailer profit or customer service.
The second question concerned the model's additional contribution. The same structured-data 
planner performed almost identically in M5 and slightly better in RetailNet. Most of the 
inventory-performance gain over conventional ordering belongs to the numerical planning 
capability. The third question concerned role reliability. Supplier reading was completely 
accurate in the controlled messages, while business-priority selection was less reliable, 
particularly  for  RetailNet  promotion.  The  model's  demonstrated  interpretation  strength 
therefore coexisted with a limitation in applying a declared policy.
7.2 The practical conclusion
A small local language model can be a useful replenishment partner when its responsibility is 
clear. In this study, it successfully converted the tested supplier information into usable rules 
and participated in a complete guarded process. The evidence supports that bounded language 
role. It does not establish that the model independently predicts demand or calculates better 
quantities than a capable numerical planner.
For managers, the recommended starting point is a reliable planner with clear information 
definitions  and  supplier  limits.  Language  assistance  can  then  be  evaluated  where  text 
interpretation creates additional work. Firm policy mappings should remain explicit, and 
actual field variation should be tested before routine release is expanded. The commercial 

LLM AGENTS FOR RETAIL REPLENISHMENT | M5 & RETAILNET
Page 57 of 70
case should include review effort and the cost of producing structured information, since 
those possible benefits and burdens were not measured here.
The CPU-only implementation shows that this assessment was feasible with Qwen2.5 1.5B 
and a modest cloud computer. The model was pretrained and used for inference; LightGBM 
was fitted to the retail histories. This gives the study a practical local-computing context 
without implying that the same latency, reliability or economics will hold at every production 
scale.
7.3 A focused next research step
The first priority for future work is an operational comparison of supplier-information 
preparation. Real messages, staff review time and exception outcomes could show whether 
the accurate reading role reduces work compared with the current structured process. The 
same  numerical  planner  should  be  retained  in  that  comparison  so  that  any  additional 
language-model value can be attributed clearly.
A  second  priority  is  understanding  promotion-policy  selection.  The  current  role  made 
inconsistent choices despite accurate supplier fields. A separately registered study could 
compare clearer event interpretation, direct policy mappings and alternative local model 
configurations. A third is extending the operating horizon and the panel, including more 
varied product lives and realistic commercial costs. That would address stock carry-over and 
transfer questions that the two weekly simulations cannot resolve.
The overall conclusion is therefore positive and specific. The study demonstrates a working 
local agent-assisted replenishment process and reliable supplier reading in its controlled 
setting. Its strongest inventory gains came from the numerical planner. Future development 
should preserve that strength and test language-model assistance where its interpretation 
capability can add a separately measured business benefit.

A.1 Measures and aggregation
The simulator's daily inventory operating cost is 0.02 times closing stock, plus five times

primary normalized policy measure. These coefficients are assumptions. Purchase spend is 
recorded separately, and the index is not a complete retailer profit calculation. Actual 
acquisition margins, staff effort, cloud charges and closing-stock commercial value are not 
estimated.
For  each  dataset,  scenario,  policy  and  simulation  seed,  the  two  weekly  episodes  are 
aggregated using their totals. Fulfilment divides total sales by total demand. The expiry share 
divides total expired quantity by total opening stock plus receipts, counting the opening 
inventory from each independent weekly reset. Policy means average the resulting seed 
measures. Overall headline means then give equal weight to the three scenarios.

Appendix D. Short glossary
An agent role is a defined model-assisted responsibility within the workflow, such as supplier 
reading or review. A language model is the pretrained system that produces the role's 
response. The three roles in this study use one model family. Ollama is the local serving 
software. Inference means using existing model weights; fitting or training adjusts a model 
using data. LightGBM is the numerical forecasting family fitted to the retail histories.
A store–product series is the sales history of one product at one store. A portfolio groups 
thirty such series for a joint replenishment decision. A weekly origin is the date at which the 
forecast and simulation episode begin. A simulation seed identifies one declared set of 
demand and supply draws. A lead time is the delay before a permitted purchase can arrive. A 
delivery pipeline contains goods that have been accepted for future arrival but are not yet on 
hand.
A carton rule restricts quantities to package multiples. A minimum order specifies the 
smallest nonzero line allowed. Supplier capacity limits the total quantity dispatched by a 
supplier group. FIFO means first-in, first-out use of stock. Expiry is the end of a batch's 
hypothetical usable life in the simulator. Fulfilment is the share of the declared demand 
served. The operating-cost index is the study's synthetic assessment measure, rather than 
observed currency or retailer profit.
Source verification checks an extraction against the current authenticated contract fields. A 
release gate applies the conditions required to execute a proposal. A hold prevents a decision 
from executing; it does not automatically create an alternative order. A paired comparison 
assesses policies under the same declared operating world. A bootstrap interval summarises 
uncertainty by resampling those paired simulation seeds, with related episode data kept inside 
each seed.
